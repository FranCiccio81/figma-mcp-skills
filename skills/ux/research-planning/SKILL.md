# Research planning and interview design

Research exists to change a decision. If no answer would change what the team does, do not run it.

## When to use

- Starting discovery on a new problem space.
- Preparing customer interviews, contextual inquiry, diary studies or a survey.
- Choosing between methods under time or budget constraints.

## Inputs

- The decision or question, its owner and deadline.
- What is already known (evidence inventory).
- Target users, access to them and constraints (legal, budget, language).

## Workflow

1. **Write research questions** (what the team needs to learn), distinct from interview
   questions (what participants are asked). Three to six questions maximum.
2. **Choose methods** with [the method chooser](references/method-chooser.md): what people do
   versus say, why versus how many, current versus future behaviour.
3. **Define participants:** criteria, segments, number, exclusions, inclusion of people with
   disabilities and less digitally confident users where relevant.
4. **Write the screener:** behavioural questions, no leading answers, disqualifying conditions.
5. **Design the interview guide:**
   - open with rapport and context, then the last time they did the activity;
   - ask about specific past events ("tell me about the last time you..."), not hypotheticals;
   - use neutral prompts ("what happened next?", "how did you decide?");
   - avoid leading, double-barrelled and yes/no questions; never pitch the solution;
   - close with anything they would add.
6. **Plan consent and data protection:** purpose, recording, storage location, retention period,
   anonymisation, right to withdraw; comply with GDPR or Swiss nLPD as applicable.
7. **Plan synthesis:** note-taking template, affinity mapping, codebook, who participates, how
   findings will be labelled (`[Observed]` versus interpretation).
8. **Define deliverables and timeline.**

## Outputs

A research plan: decision and research questions, methods and rationale, participants and
screener, interview guide, consent and data handling, synthesis plan, deliverables, timeline,
risks and biases.

## Edge cases

- **"Ask users what features they want":** convert into questions about past behaviour and problems.
- **Internal proxies only (sales, support):** acceptable as a first signal, labelled as secondhand.
- **Surveys for "why":** recommend qualitative research first, then a survey to size.
- **Vulnerable participants or minors:** require additional consent and an ethics review.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Research questions map to decisions.
- [ ] The method choice is justified against alternatives.
- [ ] The interview guide asks about past behaviour and contains no leading questions.
- [ ] Consent, storage and retention are specified.
- [ ] The synthesis plan separates observations from interpretations.
